South African Durban Bunny Chow

An iconic South African street food classic born in the coastal city of Durban. A hollowed loaf of crusty white bread filled with rich, spicy mutton or lamb curry with soft potatoes, crowned with the pulled bread "virgin" stopper and pickled carrot sambal.

Prep Time 20 Mins
Cook Time 50 Mins
Origin Durban / South Africa
Heat Level 🔥 Fiery & Rich
Saved recipes persist across visits
Adjust Servings: 4 Servings

Ingredients List

Quantities scale dynamically with the buttons above.

  • Crusty unsliced white bread loaves 2 loaves (halved)
  • Mutton, lamb, or beef (cubed on bone) 2 lbs
  • Yukon gold potatoes (quartered) 4 medium
  • Durban masala / curry powder 3 tbsp
  • Coriander seeds & cumin seeds 1 tsp each
  • Fresh curry leaves 10 leaves
  • Cinnamon stick & star anise 1 each
  • Finely chopped tomatoes 2 whole
  • Garlic and ginger paste 2 tbsp
  • Sunflower oil 3 tbsp
  • Grated carrots (for vinegar sambal) 2 cups

Ingenuity Under Apartheid

Bunny Chow was invented in the 1940s by Indian migrant workers and golf caddies in Durban. Under apartheid segregation laws, non-white workers were barred from dining inside restaurants. Clever cooks hollowed loaves of bread as edible, portable takeout bowls that could be carried onto the sugar cane fields and eaten by hand without utensils.

Cooking Instructions

Technique for building a deep red Durban curry gravy and preparing the crusty bread bowl.

Step 1: Temper Whole Spices and Aromatics

Heat oil in a heavy pot over medium heat. Add the cinnamon stick, star anise, cumin seeds, and fresh curry leaves. Allow them to sizzle for 60 seconds until aromatic. Add the chopped onions and cook until golden brown. Stir in ginger and garlic paste.

Step 2: Bloom Durban Curry Powder & Sear Mutton

Add the vibrant red Durban curry powder and turmeric. Stir constantly for 45 seconds to cook out raw spices without burning. Add the cubed mutton and toss vigorously to seal all sides in the red paste.

Step 3: Simmer with Tomatoes and Potatoes

Add chopped tomatoes and 1.5 cups of water. Cover and simmer gently for 30 minutes. Add the quartered potatoes and continue cooking until the meat is fork-tender and the potatoes absorb the gravy and begin breaking down slightly to naturally thicken the sauce.

Step 4: Hollow the Bread Loaves

Take a fresh, crusty loaf of unsliced white bread. Cut it in half (or into quarters). With clean hands, scoop out the soft white bread crumb from the center, keeping the outer crust walls intact. Reserve the scooped bread ("virgin / top piece") to serve on top.

Step 5: Fill and Garnish with Carrot Sambal

Ladle the bubbling mutton curry and gravy directly into the bread cavity, allowing the gravy to soak into the bottom walls. Place the bread top back over the curry. Serve piping hot alongside grated carrot and green chili vinegar sambal. Eat purely by hand, tearing bread from the sides to dip into the gravy.
