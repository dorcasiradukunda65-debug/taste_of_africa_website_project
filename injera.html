Ethiopian Injera with Doro Wat

The quintessential communal feast of the Horn of Africa. A fermented sourdough flatbread crafted from ancient teff grain with characteristic 'eyes' (aerated pockets), served as an edible platter for tender chicken and boiled eggs braised in caramelized onions and Berbere spice.

Ferment Time 3 Days
Cook Time 1 hr 15 Mins
Origin Ethiopia & Eritrea
Heat Level 🌶️ Bold Berbere
Saved recipes persist across visits
Adjust Servings: 6 Servings

Ingredients List

Quantities scale dynamically with the buttons above.

  • Brown or ivory teff flour 3 cups
  • Filtered water (for fermentation) 3.5 cups
  • Chicken legs/thighs (skinless, scored) 6 pieces
  • Hard-boiled eggs (peeled and pierced) 6 whole
  • Red onions (pureed finely without oil) 4 large
  • Authentic Ethiopian Berbere spice blend 3 tbsp
  • Niter Kibbeh (spiced clarified butter) ½ cup
  • Fresh ginger and garlic paste 2 tbsp
  • Fresh lemon juice (for chicken brine) 2 tbsp
  • Cardamom powder (Korarima) ½ tsp

The Art of Gursha

Injera serves simultaneously as a platter, tablecloth, and utensil. Diners tear pieces of injera with the right hand to pinch bites of spicy wats. Feeding a bite directly into a companion's mouth—known as Gursha—is an act of profound love, friendship, and hospitality.

Cooking Instructions

Two-part authentic guide: Fermenting the teff batter and braising the legendary Doro Wat stew.

Step 1: Ferment the Teff Batter (3-Day Process)

In a large glass bowl, combine teff flour and filtered water. Whisk until smooth, cover with a breathable cheesecloth, and let ferment at warm room temperature for 3 days. Tiny bubbles and an aromatic, pleasantly sour aroma will emerge. Discard the liquid that rises to the top and whisk the remaining thick batter.

Step 2: Cook Injera on the Mitad / Crêpe Pan

Heat a non-stick flat crêpe pan or traditional clay mitad over medium heat. Pour ¾ cup of batter starting in a spiral from the outer edge inward. When thousands of small craters ("eyes") form across the entire surface (about 45 seconds), cover with a high-domed lid and steam for 2 minutes until cooked through without flipping. Cool on clean woven mats.

Step 3: Dry-Caramelize the Onions (The Secret to Doro Wat)

Place the finely minced red onions into a dry heavy Dutch oven with NO oil or water. Sauté on medium-low heat, stirring constantly for 30–40 minutes. The moisture will evaporate, turning the onions into a rich, sweet, mahogany purée. This forms the foundation of great Doro Wat.

Step 4: Bloom Berbere and Simmer Chicken

Add Niter Kibbeh (spiced clarified butter) to the caramelized onions, followed by garlic, ginger, and 3 tablespoons of Berbere spice. Fry the spices for 5 minutes until intensely fragrant. Add the lemon-marinated chicken pieces and 1 cup of chicken broth. Simmer on low for 40 minutes.

Step 5: Add Hard-Boiled Eggs & Final Presentation

Pierce the peeled hard-boiled eggs with a fork so the red gravy penetrates the whites. Nestle the eggs into the stew, sprinkle ground Korarima (false cardamom), and simmer for 10 more minutes. Unfurl a wide sheet of Injera on a large communal tray, ladle the Doro Wat into the center, and serve with rolled extra injera.
