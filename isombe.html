Traditional Rwandan Isombe

The quintessential comfort food of Rwanda. Tender young cassava leaves pounded in a wooden mortar (iseke), slow-boiled with green leeks, sweet aubergines, and aromatic beef bone broth, enriched with creamy roasted groundnut (peanut) paste.

Prep Time 30 Mins
Simmer Time 1 hr 15 Mins
Origin Rwanda / East Africa
Profile Earthy & Creamy

Ingredients List

Quantities scale dynamically with the buttons above.

  • Young cassava leaves (finely pounded / Sombe) 1 kg
  • Pure roasted peanut butter (smooth / unflavored) ¾ cup
  • Large leeks (white and pale green parts, sliced) 2 stalks
  • Purple eggplants (peeled and diced) 2 medium
  • Green bell peppers (finely chopped) 2 whole
  • Garlic cloves (crushed) 5 cloves
  • Rich bone broth or smoked dried fish 3 cups
  • Red palm oil or sunflower oil 3 tbsp
  • Spring onions (chopped) 1 bunch
  • Sea salt and black pepper To taste

Heart of the Thousand Hills

In Rwandan households and festive gatherings, Isombe is always prepared with reverence. Young cassava leaves are harvested fresh from terraced hillsides, meticulously washed, and pounded into a fine emerald paste. It is traditionally paired with boiled green plantains (Igitoki) or steamed sweet potatoes.

Cooking Instructions

Traditional technique for tenderizing cassava greens and incorporating the groundnut cream.

Step 1: Prep and Pound the Cassava Leaves

Rinse the fresh young cassava leaves several times in cold water to remove soil. Place small batches into a large wooden mortar and pestle (iseke) and pound thoroughly until reduced to a fine, shred-free green pulp.

Step 2: Initial Boil with Aromatics

Transfer the pounded cassava greens into a large pot. Add 4 cups of water or bone broth, diced eggplants, chopped leeks, and bell peppers. Bring to a rapid boil, reduce heat to medium-low, and simmer uncovered for 45 minutes until the bitterness mellows and greens soften.

Step 3: Sauté the Garlic and Seasoning

In a separate skillet, warm the palm oil over medium heat. Sauté the crushed garlic and spring onions for 3 minutes until aromatic. Pour the seasoned oil into the bubbling cassava pot and stir thoroughly to distribute the flavors.

Step 4: Incorporate the Roasted Peanut Paste

In a bowl, dilute the roasted peanut paste with ½ cup of warm broth until smooth and pourable. Stir the peanut cream into the Isombe. Reduce the flame to low and simmer gently for 25 minutes, stirring frequently so the bottom does not scorch. The sauce will turn silky, rich, and deeply fragrant.

Step 5: Traditional Serving Accompaniments

Ladle the warm Isombe onto wide serving platters alongside freshly steamed green matooke plantains (Igitoki), boiled cassava tubers, or Rwandan white rice.
