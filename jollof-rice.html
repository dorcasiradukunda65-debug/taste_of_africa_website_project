Smoky West African Jollof Rice

The crown jewel of West African celebrations. Long-grain parboiled rice slow-simmered in a vibrant purée of roasted red bell peppers, plum tomatoes, onions, and fiery Scotch bonnet peppers, infused with garlic, ginger, thyme, and bay leaves.

Prep Time 20 Mins
Cook Time 45 Mins
Origin Senegambia / West Africa
Heat Level Medium-Hot
Saved recipes persist across visits
Adjust Servings: 4 Servings

Ingredients List

Quantities scale dynamically with the buttons above.

  • Long-grain parboiled rice (washed) 3 cups
  • Ripe plum / Roma tomatoes 5 whole
  • Red bell peppers (Tatashe) 3 large
  • Scotch bonnet peppers (Ata Rodo) 2 peppers
  • Red onions (divided) 2 medium
  • Tomato paste ½ cup
  • Rich chicken or beef stock 3.5 cups
  • Dried thyme leaves 1 tbsp
  • Curry powder 1 tbsp
  • Bay leaves 3 leaves
  • Vegetable oil / Red palm oil ½ cup
  • Butter (for finish gloss) 2 tbsp

Cultural Significance

Jollof rice traces its ancestral heritage to the Wolof Empire of Senegal (where it is known as Thieboudienne). As it spread across the West African coastline, nations developed beloved variations—sparking the famous and celebratory "Jollof Wars" between Nigeria, Ghana, and Sierra Leone.

Cooking Instructions

Follow these traditional techniques to achieve the authentic taste, fluffy grains, and signature party smoke.

Step 1: Roast and Blend the Pepper Base (Obeyata)

Roughly chop the plum tomatoes, red bell peppers, Scotch bonnets, and one of the red onions. Place them in a blender and purée until completely smooth. Pour the mixture into a wide heavy pot and boil on medium-high heat for 12–15 minutes until the excess water evaporates and the mixture forms a thick, concentrated red paste.

Step 2: Fry the Aromatics and Tomato Paste

In a large heavy-bottom Dutch oven, heat your vegetable oil over medium heat. Thinly slice the second red onion and fry for 4 minutes until golden and fragrant. Stir in the concentrated tomato paste and fry constantly for 5–7 minutes to remove tartness and caramelize the sugars.

Step 3: Simmer the Stew Base with Spices

Add the boiled pepper purée into the fried tomato paste. Add dried thyme, curry powder, crushed bouillon seasoning, and 3 bay leaves. Cover and simmer on low heat for 15 minutes until fragrant oil pools on top of the sauce. Scoop out 1 cup of this stew and reserve it for the final steam stage.

Step 4: Incorporate the Rice and Steam Cook

Pour in the rich chicken stock and bring to a rolling boil. Taste and adjust salt. Add the washed, drained long-grain parboiled rice. Stir gently once so the grains are submerged in the sauce. Seal the top of the pot tightly with aluminum foil before putting on the lid—this traps all steam to cook the rice evenly without making it mushy. Reduce heat to the lowest simmer for 30 minutes.

Step 5: The "Party Smoke" Finish

Remove the lid and foil. Gently fold in the reserved stew and 2 tablespoons of butter for a glossy sheen. Increase the heat to medium-high for 3–5 minutes, allowing the bottom layer of rice to lightly char. This imparts the legendary wood-smoke flavor beloved in West African celebration parties. Serve with sweet fried dodo (plantains).
